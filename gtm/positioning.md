# ReviewAsk — Positioning

**Tagline:** More 5-star reviews. Less awkward asking.
**Price:** $39/mo, flat. No contract. Goal: 10 paying subscribers.

## One-liner
ReviewAsk sends your customers a 1-tap text or email after every job asking for a Google review, so you get more reviews without having to ask in person.

## ICP: who buys fastest (in order)
1. **Owner-operated home services (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage door, roofing, cleaning, pest control).** 1–15 employees, owner answers the phone, 10–80 Google reviews, competitors nearby with 150+.
   - Why fast: Google Maps ranking is a big source of leads. One extra job a month covers $39 many times over. The owner makes the decision alone.
   - Signal: fewer reviews than the top 3 in the Maps pack, last review more than 30 days old, a website with a "Call now" button.
2. **Dental offices, med spas, salons, barbers.** They book appointments, so they have a steady flow of customers to ask, and the front desk forgets to.
   - Why: high volume of customers, and "Near me" searches decide where people go.
   - Watch out: dental may have a practice manager and existing software (Weave, etc.). Salons are faster and cheaper to close.
3. **Real estate agents (solo or small team).** Each closing is worth a lot, and reviews on their Google Business Profile help win listings.
   - Why slower: low volume (5–20 closings a year), so $39/mo is harder to justify. Pitch "ask your whole past-client list once, then after every closing."

**Skip for now:** franchises (corporate controls this), restaurants (volume is high but customer contact info is rare), businesses with 300+ reviews (they already have a system).

## Pain (in their words)
- "I forget to ask. When I do ask, it feels awkward."
- "Customers say they'll leave a review and then never do."
- "The guy down the street has 400 reviews and I have 40."
- "Podium/Birdeye want $300+/mo and a contract for something I only use for one thing."
- "One bad review sits at the top because I don't get enough good ones to bury it."

## What it does (keep it this simple)
1. Add a customer (name and phone/email) by hand, by CSV, or with a "text this number" shortcut after a job.
2. ReviewAsk sends one short, friendly message with a direct link to your Google review form. If there's no reply, it sends one reminder.
3. The dashboard shows how many requests went out, how many were clicked, and how many new reviews came in.

## Compliance notes (read before writing copy)
- **No review gating.** Google's policy says you can't filter out unhappy customers before asking for a review. Send the same request to every customer. Marketing copy can say "your customers," not "only happy customers." A private feedback option is fine if the Google link is always shown too.
- **SMS:** only text customers who gave their number for the job. Include the business name and "Reply STOP to opt out." US business texting needs A2P 10DLC registration through the SMS provider.
- **Cold email (our outreach):** CAN-SPAM. Use a real sender name, a physical address, and an opt-out line, and honor opt-outs fast. Send from a secondary domain (e.g. getreviewask.com) so the main domain's reputation is protected.

## Objections and replies
**"I already ask customers in person."**
> Good. How many actually follow through? Usually 1 in 10. A text with the link right there, sent an hour after the job, gets a lot more of them to do it. Try it on your next 20 jobs and compare.

**"$39 is too much / money is tight."**
> If it brings in one extra job a month from Maps, it's paid for many times over. There's no contract, so cancel anytime. The first 14 days are free.

**"I don't have time to set up software."**
> It takes about 10 minutes. Send me a list of last month's customers and I'll load it and send the first batch for you.

**"I use Podium / Birdeye / my CRM already does this."**
> If it's working, keep it. If you're paying $300/mo mostly for review requests, this does that part for $39.

**"What if someone leaves a bad review?"**
> Most unhappy customers leave a review anyway. Asking everyone means the happy majority shows up too, so one bad review gets buried instead of sitting on top.

**"Is texting customers legal / will it annoy them?"**
> It's one text plus at most one reminder, only to customers you just served, with an opt-out. That's normal follow-up, not marketing.

**"I'll think about it."**
> Sure. Want me to set up the free trial now so it's ready when you finish your next job? Nothing is charged for 14 days.

**"How do I know it works?"**
> The dashboard counts new Google reviews since you started. If the number hasn't gone up in 30 days, tell me and I'll refund the month.
